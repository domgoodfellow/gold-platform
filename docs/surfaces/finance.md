# Finance surfaces

Workspace and feature surface specifications. System-wide rules, including **Core rules**, are in [docs/ui-system.md](../ui-system.md). Before changing a surface here, check `docs/decisions/` for ADRs that name it.

## Finance Dashboard header

The Finance Dashboard is the approved exception: its reporting period leads its header controls, followed by Customize dashboard, Configure and its financial actions, with the one primary action last. The header stays one row: below wide desktop, the secondary actions (New quote, Record payment) and Configure move into one More menu, and on a phone Customize dashboard moves there too, while the reporting period and the primary action stay visible. Choosing a month applies it; Apply remains where there is room.

## Finance Dashboard

Finance Dashboard reads, in order: Financial overview (Payments received for the selected month, Outstanding, Overdue and Quoted, not invoiced; amounts per currency for `report.read`, exact counts otherwise, never one figure across currencies); Needs attention, offering only categories with records — Overdue (unpaid past due), Due soon (unpaid and due from now through the seventh day ahead, so never also overdue), Ready to invoice (priced Work without an invoice) and Card payments only when the card module is on without a usable processor account — or one all-clear line; Financial activity from recorded Finance events, each linked to its Work item or the catalog, with corrections marked; then, as Dashboard widgets, Payments received (line by received date, net of corrections), Receivables aging (current balances by band, per currency), Invoiced and recorded costs (top customers for the month; no margin drawn) for `report.read` only, and Invoices by status (overdue, unpaid not overdue, settled, adding up to all issued invoices) for every Finance reader. The default composition on a wide Dashboard is the overview across the grid, Needs attention (one third) beside Payments received (two thirds), Receivables aging beside Financial activity, then Invoiced and recorded costs beside Invoices by status; the overview, Needs attention and Payments received cannot be hidden. Before the first priced Work, invoice or payment the Dashboard keeps its shell: each overview tile reads None with one shared line saying nothing is recorded yet, the Needs attention slot is a compact Getting started panel holding the one primary next step (Set up pricing while the price book is empty, then Pricing configured with New quote), Financial activity appears once a Finance event is recorded, the reporting charts are not drawn, and Customize dashboard remains available. In that first run the header has no primary action, so nothing competes with Getting started; authorized operations (New quote) stay as secondary actions. Afterwards one header action is primary (New invoice where permitted, then Record payment, then New quote), the others secondary; New invoice and Record payment appear once something is recorded. Quotes, Invoices and Payments share the `CollectionToolbar`: quick scopes (Invoices: All, Outstanding, Due soon, Overdue, Settled; Quotes: All, Not invoiced, Invoiced; Payments: All, Receipts, Corrections), search, Filters (contact, dates, due date, payment source) shown as removable chips with Reset, sort, and URL-backed pagination with database counts. Narrow invoice cards keep identity, due date, status and outstanding amount. Finance search and financial reporting stay at `/finance/search` and `/finance/reports`.

## Finance transactions

Finance transactions use purpose-built guarded dialogs over the shared transaction grammar (`components/patterns/transaction-review.tsx`: record picker, record summary, line items, money totals, confirmation, result), not a drawer. New quote defaults to Customer quote for `price.manage`: choose a Contact and catalog quantities, review the total and build an existing Commercial Proposal version without Work. Price existing Work remains available under `work_item.price`; a Work record opens that source directly. Customer quotes open the Contact's commercial terms and retain the existing sent/accepted/declined lifecycle. Finance lists both sources with exact counts, search, sorting and pagination. Ready to invoice includes priced Work and accepted proposals only. Changing source guards unsaved lines; an empty catalog offers Set up pricing. New invoice reviews eligible priced Work, its lines, total and dates, then requires a separate confirmation step before canonical issuance (the server refuses an unconfirmed issue); Work that already has an invoice offers that invoice instead. Record payment shows the invoice number, customer, currency, total, already paid and outstanding; a receipt and a correction or refund are chosen explicitly, a correction is entered as a positive amount, shown in the danger tone, previewed and confirmed before it is recorded as its own negative entry, and earlier entries are never changed. Manual entry states that it is not processor-confirmed. Refusals keep typed input and move focus to the reason; unsaved input asks before discard; focus returns to the trigger. Work quote and invoice links open `/work/[workItemId]?tab=financials`; customer quotes open the Contact’s commercial terms and proposal invoices use Finance’s invoice inspector. Catalog & Pricing is `/finance/catalog`, an operating page with identity-rich rows edited in place; Add price item is always the price book's primary control (beside its heading, also when empty), adding a price item creates no Work or Work type, and quoting snapshots its price onto the proposal or Work. Each item offers an optional Delivery Work type, including No job delivery, saved separately from pricing; linked active types offer Create Work from the item. Its header links managers to Payment settings (`/settings/payments`). The old `/finance/configuration`, `/finance/configuration/pricing` and `/settings/commerce` addresses only redirect to Customer payments (General for a member who may not open it), the pricing address as a plain HTTP redirect with a relative Location so `#getting-paid` survives; General forwards its own old `#getting-paid` anchor the same way. Payment configuration remains permission-gated and billing-entitled, and the trading currency has one editor, General's Regional settings. Aggregate financial values require `report.read`; pricing, issuance, payment and organization configuration permissions remain independent.

## Recurring items

Catalog & Pricing lists recurring items beneath the one-off price book when the business has the Recurring items feature: name, kind (Plan or Add-on for GildOps' plan items, otherwise the unit), the prices in force per currency and interval, and Not offered or Retired when the item is not offered. New recurring item (with `price.manage`) asks for a name, what it is charged per and a description, then opens the item. In GildOps' own organization it also asks the kind — Recurring item, GildOps plan or GildOps add-on — and this is the only place a GildOps plan or add-on is created (ADR-057): it starts Not offered, with an empty switch list.

An item's page (`finance/catalog/[itemId]`) shows its prices in force in the header and, in order: Prices (the full history, newest first, with Current on the price in force; Record price asks for amount, currency, how often it is charged — every month, every year or once — and when it starts); Add-ons (offered items, with Offer and Remove; a plan carries add-ons, an add-on carries none); and, for an item that sells a GildOps plan, What it switches on (products, feature groups, features added or left out and allowances: editable — with Edit for whether it is billed and its order — by members who also hold the Product Registry permission `platform.catalogue`, otherwise a read-only Includes list), Usage beyond the allowance (the price per unit of each metered allowance, recorded to six decimal places beyond the currency's own, never starting in the past) and, for members with `subscription.read`, Subscribers (each business holding the plan, opening its subscription). Members with `cost.read` get an Economics action that opens the plan in package economics. For GildOps' plan items each approved price shows whether it is ready for checkout with GildOps' billing provider, with Prepare for checkout. Edit changes name, unit, description and whether the item is offered; for a plan item that renames the plan everywhere it is shown. Prices are never edited or deleted: a new price is recorded with its own start. A recurring item is sold through Subscriptions and is never offered on quotes, invoices, Work or booking.

## Expected costs and margin

Each one-off item's name in Catalog & Pricing opens its page (`finance/catalog/[itemId]`): its price in the header, Delivery (the Work type that delivers it, linked to that type's Pricing tab for members who configure Work, or Not delivered as work), and, for members with `cost.read`, Expected cost. Expected cost lists the item's own cost lines per unit (materials, parts, supplier charges), added and removed with `cost.manage`; then, when a Work type delivers it, that type's stages with their expected time and cost per hour, edited on the Work type; then Price, Expected cost and Expected margin with its share of the price. A cost in another currency than the price leaves the margin Unknown and says why. Nothing here changes a quote, job or invoice.

Financial reports shows, for members with `cost.read` and a trading currency, Expected margin by item (each priced item, the type that delivers it, price, expected cost and expected margin) and Work types: recorded and expected. The second lists each type that delivers priced items or has work: jobs finished in the month (moved into a finishing stage), their average price, recorded cost and margin per job in the trading currency, the expected margin per job (a range when the type delivers several items), the jobs open now and the expected margin on that open work. One line says what Finished, Open and Expected mean. Recorded figures come from the jobs (`work_type_results`); expected ones from the assumptions and are stored nowhere.

## Finance pricing planning

Financial reports contains a compact `OperationalPanel` for unsaved Economics & pricing planning beneath recorded financial activity. Up to three independently editable scenario fieldsets wrap according to the available container width. Shared inputs, `CurrencyField` and `formatMoney` own input grammar, hydration-safe currency selection and monetary display. Each scenario identifies estimated costs, suggested selling price, estimated gross profit/margin and an optional current catalog unit comparison. Missing assumptions remain Unknown; zero assumptions require an explanation; zero selling price has undefined margin. Currency changes clear monetary inputs, and foreign-currency comparisons state why they cannot be calculated. Current catalog prices remain visibly recorded context. The panel states that scenarios are lost on leaving/reload and update no financial records; no publish control is offered.

## Subscriptions

**Subscriptions** (`/finance/subscriptions`, `subscription.read`) is in Gild Finance for every business (ADR-057). It lists each subscription the business keeps with one of its own customers: the customer, what it is for (the primary recurring item and how many add-ons), the price per billing interval (marked agreed when a line has an agreed price), its status (Starts later, Active, Ending, Ended) and, on cards, who invoices it (Finance, or Stripe for a GildOps subscription a business pays by card) and since when. New subscription (with `subscription.manage`) chooses a customer, one of the business's active recurring items, monthly or yearly billing, the currency, the start (and an optional end), the quantity and an optional agreed price; empty uses the item's list price, and an item with no list price in those terms is subscribed to only at an agreed price. GildOps sells its plans here exactly the same way.

A subscription opens its record (`/finance/subscriptions/{subscriptionId}`): status, billing interval and currency and who invoices it in the header, with Customer (with `customer.read`) and End subscription (with `subscription.manage`). The record shows what is in force or scheduled (item, add-on, quantity, price with Agreed marked, list price on cards, from and last day) and the total per interval (Unknown, never zero, when a line is unpriced). With `subscription.manage`: Change item (from a date; the current item ends there), Add add-on (only an add-on Catalog & Pricing offers for what it is for), Change price (an agreed price from now, or back to the list price), End add-on and End subscription (choosing the last day of service). Tabs: Invoices (Invoice next period with `invoice.issue`, for a subscription Finance invoices; each period once, at the prices in force when it starts, with no proration) and History (every line, every agreed price).

Where a subscription's lines sell GildOps plans to a customer linked to a provisioned business, the record also shows that business: Business in Administration (with `platform.organizations`), service availability in the header, and tabs What it switches on, Allowances (Change an allowance), Usage prices (Agree a usage price for a meter; empty returns it to the plan's), Stripe (the billing provider's subscription, items and deliveries; Set up agreed price for checkout), Economics (with `cost.read`) and Per-product terms (Record a term). Each change is a new dated record, confirmed in a toast.

## GildOps' Costs and Economics

These two destinations belong to the unlisted operator Product, which only GildOps' own plan includes (ADR-057). Each page proves the Finance area and its feature first; the database answers only to the Finance permission in GildOps' organization, never to platform authority.

**Costs** (`/finance/costs`, `cost.read`) has three views: Supplier rates (per supplier, service and region, with history, overlap flagged; Add rate with `cost.manage`), Usage costs (each usage event's known cost, customer charge and cost state, filtered by date, business, meter, supplier and state) and Infrastructure (the month's recorded cost components per business or shared; Record cost with `cost.manage`).

**Economics** (`/finance/economics`, `cost.read`) has Portfolio (this month per business: owed, known cost, known contribution, known margin and cost coverage, opening each business's cost to serve at `/finance/economics/{business}`, which links to its subscription) and Packages (`/finance/economics/packages`: the package simulator over a plan, a draft scenario or a business's agreed price; New draft scenario, Margin targets and Usage assumptions with `cost.manage`). Usage beyond an allowance is priced by the one usage-price rule (an agreed price on the subscription, else an add-on's, else the plan's). Neither is accounting profit; shared and unallocated infrastructure are shown beside, never inside, a business's cost.

## Invoices of a subscription

A subscription's invoices are ordinary Finance invoices with the Subscription source: issued from the record for a period, or reported by Stripe for a GildOps subscription a business pays by card. A Stripe invoice keeps its Stripe number and page, lists what Stripe charged (lines, tax, and discounts and other adjustments as one line, so it totals what Stripe says is due), records Stripe's payment and any refund as payments, and is marked Void when Stripe voids it; a void invoice is owed by no one. Its inspector opens the subscription and the Stripe invoice.
